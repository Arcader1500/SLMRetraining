# Source-grounding audit and next-experiment decision

Date: 2026-11-17. Decision: preserve the completed fine-tuning demonstration; do not extend the current ten-section task unchanged. Prefer a short, evidence-backed extraction task. Reject the proposed `stindardlogic/medical-clinical-reasoning-sft-100k` revision as a training/generalization benchmark.

## Scope and method

This is an assistant-performed source-consistency review of **all 30** frozen v2 validation examples, their reference JSON, and the saved `finetuned_v2 / gen_rp_1.08` predictions. It is not clinician adjudication. “Unsupported” means unsupported by the note actually supplied to the model; it does not assert that a claim is impossible in the real patient. Findings identify concrete errors and omissions, rather than exhaustively labeling every atomic fact. Therefore case-level counts below are not estimates of clinical precision, recall, or safety. Baseline predictions were not separately adjudicated in this review.

Inputs:

- [Frozen validation JSONL](../../../prepared_clinical_dataset/eeff5efa1619f198/validation.jsonl).
- [Fine-tuned predictions](../../../results_v2/comparison/finetuned__gen_rp_1.08/generations.json), including the two invalid outputs as raw text.
- [Generation diagnostics](../../../results_v2/comparison/finetuned__gen_rp_1.08/generation_diagnostics.json).
- [Saved case scores](../../../results_v2/comparison/case_comparison.csv) and [aggregate scores](../../../results_v2/comparison/summary_comparison.csv).

All 30 original validation records were independently fetched from `AmareshHebbar/clinical-summarizer-sft` at revision `4ca20e0a38cc9235045c973b5503d49952fd1fac`. Their message hashes and user-note text match the stored source hashes and notes. The incomplete endings discussed below were already present in that source dataset. No generation was repaired, and no frozen artifact was modified.

## What the audit establishes

| Observation | Evidence and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Useful simple extraction is learned | Age and sex agree with the source in **30/30** predictions after normalizing age units and male/man/female/woman. This includes the readable prefixes of the two invalid outputs. This is a small positive control, not an independent before/after benchmark of demographics. |
| Formatting improved strongly | **28/30** outputs parse and have all ten canonical top-level keys. The two other outputs loop and hit the 2,048-token budget. |
| Reports still need factual correction | **30/30** have at least one unsupported assertion, contradiction, incorrect event relation, or misassigned field documented in the ledger below. This does not mean every fact in those reports is wrong. |
| Errors exceed wording differences | They include antibiotic susceptibility reversal, invented diagnoses and measurements, negated symptoms presented as positive, and drug doses copied to the wrong drug. |
| Reference quality is imperfect | At least **7/30** have clear reference defects: cases 1030, 362, 1791, 390, 111, 1913, and 1175. Other rows have ambiguity, speculative rationale, temporal compression, or residual placeholder text; these are separately identified below. |
| Source context is incomplete | At least **28/30** notes visibly end in an unfinished word, phrase, or sentence. Case 978 is complete; case 1913 ends at a complete clause without final punctuation and was excluded from this conservative count. This is a property of the supplied data, not evidence that preparation truncated model tokens. |
| Current scores do not isolate clinical competence | Strict F1 penalizes age units, synonyms, paraphrase, field placement, and reference defects; soft F1 handles some wording differences but cannot establish source truth, negation, chronology, or correct event linkage. |

The two invalid reports contain substantive errors before looping. Merely making them parseable would leave those errors intact. Conversely, correct content in an invalid report receives zero whole-object fact credit under the current evaluator.

## Why the current run still has value

The saved evaluation demonstrates learning of a structured extraction behavior and source-specific facts, with strict mean F1 rising from 0.49% to 18.93% and soft mean F1 from 2.09% to 30.74% under the same generated-only penalty. The base model already produces fence-stripped JSON in 22/30 cases; fine-tuning raises this to 28/30 and teaches the canonical top-level fields. These are meaningful experimental outcomes.

However, a valid report is not a faithful report. Full ten-section extraction combines entity recognition, negation, event segmentation, chronology, clinical categorization, summarization, and medication-event linkage. The target annotations often blur those boundaries. More epochs on the same targets are a weak next investment. This evidence cannot separate model capacity from limited/diverse training data, annotation noise, and task complexity, and does not establish a capability ceiling for Gemma 270M.

## Audit of the proposed 100k dataset

Source: [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/stindardlogic/medical-clinical-reasoning-sft-100k). Audited revision: `c01f314cb453876617c407cad1c8557be2a5d09e`.

The complete JSONL was streamed and parsed, rather than relying on the viewer sample. Full-file SHA-256: `95625116118d5f587cfcfb31321a3c4e55a13b7219e5da1e861edbd67fb7f6ed`. File size: 655,694,817 bytes. [Machine-readable counts and per-example hashes](dataset_candidate_audit_2026-11-17.json).

| Quantity | Full-file result |
| --- | ---: |
| Rows | 100,000 |
| Distinct exact prompts | 15 |
| Distinct prompts after case/whitespace normalization | 15 |
| Distinct exact answers | 15 |
| Distinct exact prompt-answer pairs, excluding IDs/metadata | 15 |
| Repetitions per distinct prompt-answer pair | 6,666 or 6,667 |
| Assistant response length, characters | min 4,315; median 6,003; max 8,649 |

99,985 rows add no new prompt-answer pair. The card labels the dataset synthetic; it does not document independent clinical adjudication or a specific generating model in the inspected card. The unique examples span broad clinical advice and documentation, rather than a focused extraction task. In the discharge-summary example, the response adds comorbidities, history, and measurements absent from its short input. Such elaboration conflicts with source-faithful extraction. Long responses also make this a poor match for a compact, easily scored demonstration.

Do not use a random row split: it would almost certainly put the same clinical content in training and evaluation. Grouping by unique prompt prevents leakage but leaves only 15 scenarios. This revision is unsuitable for demonstrating generalization; it could at most serve as a memorization exercise. This is a finding about the audited revision, not a claim that every future revision will be identical.

## Recommended next experiment

Continue the extraction direction with a **new, narrower task and a clean evaluation**, while preserving all v2 artifacts. A suitable candidate is symptom assertion extraction: copy symptom spans and label each as present or absent, with a short supporting source quote. Restrict the initial task to symptoms asserted about the current patient; define how to handle history, family history, uncertainty and temporal change before annotation. Use short note excerpts and short outputs, and reject ambiguous examples from the first pilot.

For example, an input containing “Reports chest pain. No fever.” would target two symptom mentions with present/absent labels and their evidence spans. A compact JSON representation could use `text`, `status`, and `evidence`. This is a proposed task; no new labels, dataset version, or training run was created by this audit.

Why this is preferable: source evidence is inspectable; unsupported names and reversed negation can be measured; outputs can be roughly 50–150 tokens rather than many hundreds; and the objective demonstrates a practical extraction behavior without asking the model to invent medical knowledge or recommend treatment. Age/sex alone would be too weak a demo given the current adapter's 30/30 result and the likely strength of simple baselines.

Before training:

1. Create a small annotation guide and inspect 50–100 diverse candidate examples. Label against source text, not by blindly reusing the noisy ten-section JSON. Existing raw notes can supply candidates, but truncation and near duplicates need handling.
2. Deduplicate/group related cases before splitting. The existing 30 cases are development data now. Reserve a fresh, reviewed test set from unused data, after cross-split exact/near-duplicate checks.
3. Run the unfine-tuned model and a simple extraction baseline on the narrow task first. If performance is already near ceiling, increase meaningful difficulty or choose another task before spending on training.
4. Freeze separately reviewed development/test targets. A reasonable initial scale is 2,000–5,000 diverse training examples, 100 development examples and 100–200 test examples, subject to unique-data and labeling availability; these are planning estimates, not audited existing resources.
5. Score entity-plus-assertion precision/recall/F1, unsupported output rate, negation errors and JSON validity separately. Evidence-span existence can be checked programmatically but does not prove the assertion label is correct. Manually inspect a fixed sample of errors. Predeclare a useful gain over baseline (for example, at least 10 percentage points in assertion F1 without increasing unsupported outputs), with uncertainty reported on the held-out set.
6. Run one small controlled LoRA pilot, retaining the established FP32/T4 recipe unless precision is deliberately changed and recorded. Do not carry over the old schema, prompts, scores or baseline contract into the new experiment.

Recommendation: **retain v2 as a successful but limited learning experiment; stop extending the full report generator unchanged; try a short, auditable extraction task; do not adopt the proposed 100k dataset at the audited revision.**
